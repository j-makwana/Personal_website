01.1 / Founder · Product & engineering

2024 – Present

A better way to
store the summer.

Boxmate / Student storage marketplace

Moving out should not mean figuring out storage alone. I founded Boxmate to connect UW-Madison students with local hosts and built the business around what students actually needed.

Visit Boxmate ↗

The insight

230+ survey responses and customer interviews surfaced price sensitivity, low freshman awareness and demand for delivery.

The decision

I moved pricing from per-item to bundles to flat tiers, directed the UI/UX, and led a team of about 10 across the storefront, backend and host app.

The result

150+ students served and 95 hosts signed. Summer 2026 revenue reached $15.3K, up 3.5x year over year; 88% of 95 paid orders chose pickup and delivery.

A recurring problem became a product

Less searching.
More engineering.

Preparing cutsheets meant repetitive document work. I built a Python, Next.js and Claude API tool that extracts and verifies information in two passes, flags missing datasheets and keeps engineers in control with accept/reject review.

I partnered with the EPLAN team to order datasheets from X/Y exports, then added searchable job history to help reuse schematic sections and track completion.

Adopted by all six engineers across about 18 jobs a week, saving 10–15 minutes per job.

01.3 / Software engineer intern · Embedded systems

2023 – 2025

Software that meets

Credo + UW Space Science & Engineering Center

Reliable systems start with the details: a sensor that comes up correctly, a fault that recovers, a measurement you can trust. My engineering work spans customer-shipped firmware, scientific automation and hardware prototypes.

Design for recovery

At Credo, I implemented a hardware watchdog and wrote and executed I2C tests for an STM32 leak-detection product shipped to a customer, covering sensor bring-up, fault recovery and bench validation in C.

Make measurements repeatable

At UW Space Science, I replaced manual resistance testing with a Python pipeline controlling a Keithley DAQ over SCPI/TCP and logging to HDF5. I also designed and SPICE-validated a heater-amplifier PCB for the SHIS instrument.
